Ground-penetrating radar (GPR)#

A GPR antenna sends a short radar pulse into the ground and records its echoes from changes in relative permittivity, which in a soil is set mostly by the water it holds. A common-offset line, one trace every few centimetres, gives a radargram: reflectors and diffraction hyperbolas in two-way travel time, a few metres deep at 100-500 MHz.

The GPR tools are a first, simple framework: one line at a time, a single velocity for the whole line, no picking yet. They are written in the package (PyHydroGeophysX.data_processing.gpr) and need only NumPy and SciPy. The processing steps use the standard sequence also used by the open-source GPR codes GPRPy, readgssi, ImpDAR and RGPR; see Citation.

What you can do#

  • Read a line from GSSI .DZT, Sensors & Software pulseEKKO .DT1 with its .HD, MALA .rd3/.rd7 with its .rad, SEG-Y, or a .npz saved by the package.

  • Clean it up, in this order:

    • remove the slow drift under the first arrivals (dewow);

    • move time zero onto the first arrival;

    • band-pass filter (zero-phase Butterworth);

    • remove the horizontal bands that antenna ringing leaves (background removal, over the whole line or a running window of traces);

    • migrate with a constant velocity (F-K, Stolt 1978);

    • gain the late part for display (power of time, or automatic gain control).

  • Fit the radar velocity by drawing a diffraction hyperbola over the radargram, or set it from a relative permittivity (v = c / sqrt(eps)).

  • Show the line in two-way time, in depth (d = v t / 2) or, with ground elevations along the line, in elevation.

Required inputs#

One GPR line in a format listed above. A line recorded by time rather than by distance (a GSSI file without scans per metre) has no trace spacing; give one, and the traces are spaced evenly. For the elevation view, a CSV of distance, elevation in metres along the line (a pulseEKKO file’s own topography is used when it has one).

Typical outputs#

The processed line as .npz (read back with gpr.read_gpr), the radargram as drawn (.png) and the processing recipe (.json), which lists every step with its settings and what it did.

Desktop support#

Desktop Studio has a GPR page under Geophysical Data Processing, laid out like the ERT page. Data shows the original recording: open or drop a GPR file, or choose Use example, then set the signal filters and gain on the right. Every change updates the processed line automatically. Processed radargram shows the result, with velocity, depth and migration settings beside it. Trace compares one trace before and after processing; Spectrum compares their amplitude spectra with the band-pass marked. The filters remain available beside both diagnostic views.

To fit the velocity, open Processed radargram, press Fit a hyperbola, click its top and change the velocity until the dashed curve lies on it. Choose Depth or Elevation below the plot for the vertical axis. The result and save buttons stay below the settings while those settings scroll. Save results exports the processed line, its radargram and the recipe.

Use example simulates a 100 MHz line over the MODFLOW catchment model in examples/data: the model’s water content and porosity become permittivity through the CRIM mixing law, and each layer boundary reflects.

In an AQUAH run#

Give the AQUAH assistant a line under the GPR line role (gpr_file), or name it in the request (“process LINE01.DZT at 0.08 m/ns”). A .DZT, .DT1/.HD or .rd3/.rd7/.rad file is a GPR line whatever the request calls it; a SEG-Y file is one when the request is about radar and not seismic. A folder holding the MODFLOW model of examples/data stands for the simulated example line. The run makes two steps before its report:

  • load_gpr_line reads the line and draws it as recorded under raw_data/;

  • process_gpr_line runs the page’s default recipe - dewow, time zero on the first arrival, the mean trace removed, automatic gain over 25 ns - and writes gpr/<line>_processed.npz, the radargram and gpr/<line>_processing.json. gpr_params adds a band-pass (bandpass), migration (migrate) or another gain, or replaces the recipe (steps).

A depth needs a velocity, and the run uses only one somebody gave: the request’s gpr_velocity (m/ns) or gpr_permittivity, or the permittivity in a GSSI file’s header, which the report says is the value set on the instrument. Without any, the radargram stays in two-way time, migration is left out, and the report says that no depth is given and how to measure the velocity. A velocity or permittivity the request does not contain is never taken from the language model’s reading of it.

Python examples#

Ex. GPR Workflow: A Radar Line over the Hydrological Model, from Radargram to Depth runs the whole chain on the line simulated over the MODFLOW model. It processes the line, then checks time zero and the velocity against the CRIM permittivity that made it, so that a wrong choice shows as nanoseconds and metres rather than as a plausible picture. It ends with depth and elevation sections and the saved line.

On a line of your own:

from PyHydroGeophysX.data_processing import gpr

line = gpr.read_gpr("LINE01.DZT")
steps = [
    {"name": "dewow"},
    {"name": "time_zero"},
    {"name": "bandpass", "low_mhz": 100, "high_mhz": 1000},
    {"name": "background"},
    {"name": "migrate", "velocity": 0.1},
    {"name": "gain", "kind": "agc", "window_ns": 20},
]
processed = gpr.process(line, steps)
print("\n".join(processed.history))
depth = gpr.time_to_depth(processed.time_ns, 0.1)
gpr.save_profile(processed, "LINE01_processed.npz")

Optional dependencies#

None beyond NumPy and SciPy. SEG-Y is read with segyio or ObsPy when installed, and with the package’s own reader otherwise.
